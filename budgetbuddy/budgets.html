When will it run out?

The budget screen divides what you have spent by the days gone to get your pace, then projects the day you will hit the limit.

If that day falls after the period ends, it tells you the budget is safe. At 85% spent the budget is marked near its limit, and past 100% it turns red. Try it on a September budget.

Setting one up

Any period

A day, a week, a month or a year. It starts again on its own when the period ends.

Several categories

One budget can watch more than one category, such as Groceries and Eating out together.

Two warnings

Near limit from 85% spent, then Exceeded once you go past it.

Quiet if you like

Ignore reminders mutes the in-app alerts for a budget you only want to watch.

Free accounts have three budgets. Premium has no limit, and budgets can also be shown on the home Stats tab. It looks the same on Android.

Your track record

Budget Progress counts the past periods you stayed under the limit and gives you a verdict. Streaks count how many in a row, with your longest run beside it.

Each budget also has a chart of its money over time and the difference from the previous period, so “+134.00 $ vs prev” means you spent that much more than last time.

Savings goals

Set a target, a start date and, if you want one, a deadline. Then add money by hand, plan an amount each month, or skip a month, each with a note.

  • Progress in percent, an estimated finish date and the full history
  • A reminder at the end of each month, and a celebration when you get there
  • Pause, complete or archive a goal

Goals only track progress. The money itself stays in your bank account.
