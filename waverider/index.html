Wave Rider

Ride the wave. Don't run dry.

A physics-driven boat rides procedurally generated, always-moving waves as far as it can — until the fuel runs out or the sea rolls you over. Coins buy upgrades and new boats; new waters change the physics, not just the backdrop.

Features

The ground never stops moving

Two thumbs, real buoyancy, and a sea that is generated fresh every run. Easy to start, a long way to master.

Two thumbs, real physics

Right thumb throttle, left thumb brake and reverse. Get airborne and the same two buttons become nose-up and nose-down — that is the whole skill game.

The water is alive

Every wave is generated on the fly and never stops moving. No two runs give you the same swell, and nothing under you holds still while you decide.

Prop in the water

The revs climb the instant you touch the throttle, but thrust only arrives when the propeller is actually under the surface. Learn where the bite is and you keep your speed.

Fuel is the clock

Cans ride the surface ahead of you and the gap to the next one grows every time. Runs end themselves — upgrades are how you push that wall further out.

Air, flips and perfect landings

Big Air, Huge Air, chained flips and close calls all pay coins. Land with your hull matched to the water's angle and you keep every bit of speed you left with.

Made for mobile

Landscape, two thumbs, no account and no connection required. Runs last one to three minutes — pick it up for one, or for twenty.

The fleet

From a rowboat to an aircraft carrier

Seventeen boats, and none of them are a reskin. Each one is a different set of trade-offs between power, buoyancy, stability and range.

The garage

Five lanes, one honest question

Every boat tunes along the same five lanes, so you always know what a coin buys. Cards show the effect, not just the level.

Go

Engine

Raw power. More shove off the mark and a higher top speed once you are up and planing.

Hull

Hull

Weight, buoyancy and how hard a bad landing hits — the difference between a stumble and a capsize.

Bite

Propeller

How much of the engine actually reaches the water, and how fast the screw recovers once it drops back in.

Angle

Trim

Authority over your own pitch. Sets how fast you can level out before you touch back down.

Range

Fuel

Tank size. Every level pushes out the gap you can clear before the next can, and a maxed boat goes a long way past a stock one.

The lanes rename themselves to fit the boat. The rowboat tunes Oars and Rations; the jet ski has an Impeller; the pirate ship trims Sails and Rigging — and its fuel lane just reads Never runs out.
